🎄 1. Christmas – What Do We Celebrate?

Christmas is celebrated on 25 December in all six countries in this lesson. For Christians, it is a celebration of the birth of Jesus Christ. For many other people, it is also a special time for family, friends, presents, decorations and delicious food.

People decorate Christmas trees, exchange gifts, listen to Christmas music and enjoy festive meals. However, the weather, local food and special customs can be very different!

🌍 Two hemispheres – two different Christmas seasons!

In the United Kingdom, the USA, Ireland and Canada, December is in winter. In Australia and New Zealand, December is in summer!

That means some people dream of a white Christmas, while others celebrate Christmas in the sunshine, at the beach or in the garden.

🇬🇧 2. Christmas in the United Kingdom

🇬🇧

United Kingdom

WINTER ❄️

Christmas greeting: Merry Christmas! / Happy Christmas!

Christmas dinner: roast turkey, roast potatoes, vegetables, gravy and Christmas pudding. Mince pies are popular sweet pastries filled with dried fruit and spices.

Christmas crackers: colourful paper tubes that two people pull apart. They make a little bang and contain a paper crown, a joke and a small gift. People often wear the paper crowns during Christmas dinner!

Boxing Day: 26 December is a holiday in the UK. Many people relax, visit family, go for walks, watch sport or enjoy shopping sales.

Other traditions: Christmas carols, decorated trees, stockings and the King's Christmas message on television.

⭐ Fun fact: A Christmas cracker is not a biscuit! It is a festive paper tube that makes a snapping sound when pulled.

🇺🇸 3. Christmas in the United States

🇺🇸

United States of America

WINTER ❄️

Christmas greeting: Merry Christmas!

Santa Claus: Children hang Christmas stockings by the fireplace or another special place. Santa Claus brings presents during the night of 24 December.

Cookies and milk: Many children leave cookies and a glass of milk for Santa. They may also leave a carrot for his reindeer.

Christmas lights: Many houses have colourful lights and decorations. Some displays are very big and spectacular!

Christmas food: Families may eat roast turkey, ham, mashed potatoes, vegetables, cookies and other festive desserts. Traditions vary between families and regions.

Other traditions: Christmas movies, decorated trees, Christmas parades and festive music.

⭐ Fun fact: The NORAD organisation has a famous Christmas Eve tradition of tracking Santa's journey around the world!

🇦🇺 4. Christmas in Australia

🇦🇺

Australia

SUMMER ☀️

Christmas greeting: Merry Christmas!

Summer Christmas: December is a summer month in Australia. Christmas can be hot and sunny!

Beach and barbecue: Some families celebrate outdoors, have a barbecue, visit the beach or enjoy a picnic.

Christmas food: Seafood such as prawns and oysters, salads, grilled food and sometimes traditional roast dinners.

Carols by Candlelight: People gather to sing Christmas songs, often outdoors with candles. Melbourne is famous for this tradition.

Other traditions: Christmas lights, decorated trees, family lunches and summer activities.

⭐ Fun fact: Imagine wearing a Santa hat, swimming in the sea and eating Christmas lunch in the sunshine. That can be an Australian Christmas!

🇮🇪 5. Christmas in Ireland

🇮🇪

Ireland

WINTER ❄️

Christmas greeting: Nollaig Shona Duit! – Happy Christmas to you! (Irish language)

Family Christmas: Many people spend Christmas with family and friends. Christmas lights, carols and festive markets create a special atmosphere.

Christmas food: Roast turkey, ham, potatoes, vegetables and Christmas pudding are popular festive foods in many homes.

Christmas morning swim: Some brave people take a cold swim in the sea on Christmas morning. Dublin's Forty Foot swimming spot is well known for this tradition.

Wren Day: 26 December is also known as St Stephen's Day. In some Irish traditions, groups called wrenboys dress up, sing and visit homes or gather for celebrations.

Nollaig na mBan: Women's Christmas is celebrated on 6 January. It is a traditional day associated with women taking a break after the Christmas season.

⭐ Fun fact: In Ireland, you can wish someone a Happy Christmas in Irish by saying “Nollaig Shona Duit!”

🇳🇿 6. Christmas in New Zealand

🇳🇿

New Zealand

SUMMER ☀️

Christmas greeting: Merry Christmas! / Meri Kirihimete! (Māori greeting)

Summer Christmas: Christmas takes place during the warm summer season. Families may enjoy outdoor meals, picnics and beach visits.

Pōhutukawa: This beautiful native tree has bright red flowers in summer. It is often called the New Zealand Christmas tree.

Christmas food: Barbecues, fresh food, salads and pavlova – a popular dessert made with meringue, cream and fruit.

Other traditions: Christmas lights, family gatherings, carols and outdoor celebrations.

⭐ Fun fact: The pōhutukawa tree has bright red flowers around Christmas time. It is a wonderful natural Christmas symbol!

🇨🇦 7. Christmas in Canada

🇨🇦

Canada

WINTER ❄️

Christmas greeting: Merry Christmas! / Joyeux Noël! (French)

Winter celebrations: Many parts of Canada have cold winters and snow. People enjoy Christmas lights, decorated trees and winter activities.

Santa Claus: Children write letters to Santa. Canada Post has a special programme that helps Santa receive and answer children's letters.

Christmas food: Roast turkey, potatoes, vegetables, cranberry sauce, shortbread and other festive desserts.

Québec: In French-speaking Québec, some families celebrate a special Christmas Eve feast called réveillon. Traditional dishes can include tourtière, a savoury meat pie.

Boxing Day: 26 December is associated with shopping sales and family relaxation. Its official holiday status varies across Canadian provinces and territories.

⭐ Fun fact: Children can write to Santa Claus in Canada using the special postal code H0H 0H0!

🔍 8. Similarities and Differences

What is similar?

  • Christmas Day is on 25 December in all six countries.
  • Christmas trees and festive decorations are popular.
  • Many people exchange presents.
  • Families and friends often meet and share special meals.
  • Christmas songs, carols and lights are part of the festive season.
  • Santa Claus is a popular Christmas character.

📖 10. Reading – Six Christmas Stories

A Christmas Journey

Christmas is a special time in many English-speaking countries. But Christmas does not look exactly the same everywhere.

In the United Kingdom, families enjoy Christmas dinner and pull funny Christmas crackers. They may find paper crowns and jokes inside them. The next day is Boxing Day.

In the United States, children often hang stockings and leave cookies and milk for Santa Claus. Many houses have beautiful Christmas lights.

In Australia, Christmas comes in summer. Some families have a barbecue, eat seafood or spend time at the beach. In New Zealand, people celebrate in the sunshine too. The red flowers of the pōhutukawa tree make it a special Christmas symbol.

In Ireland, families enjoy Christmas together. Some people take a cold Christmas morning swim. On 26 December, Wren Day is celebrated in some communities.

In Canada, children can write letters to Santa. In Québec, some families enjoy a traditional Christmas Eve meal called réveillon.

These countries have different traditions, but they share many things: family, food, music, decorations and the joy of giving.
