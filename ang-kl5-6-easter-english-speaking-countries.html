🌷 1. What Is Easter?

Easter is an important Christian celebration. It celebrates the resurrection of Jesus Christ. Easter Sunday is the main day of the celebration.

Unlike Christmas, Easter does not have a fixed date. It usually takes place in March or April. The date changes every year.

The Easter weekend includes important days:

  • Good Friday: the Friday before Easter Sunday. Christians remember the crucifixion of Jesus.
  • Holy Saturday: the day before Easter Sunday.
  • Easter Sunday: the celebration of the resurrection of Jesus.
  • Easter Monday: a public holiday in some countries and a day for family activities in many places.

What are the symbols of Easter?

🥚Easter eggA symbol of new life.
🐰Easter BunnyA popular character who brings eggs and sweets.
🐣ChickA symbol of new life and spring.
🌷Spring flowersFlowers that represent spring and new beginnings.
🌍 A seasonal surprise!

In the UK, the USA, Ireland and Canada, Easter takes place in spring. In Australia and New Zealand, it takes place in autumn because these countries are in the Southern Hemisphere.

🇬🇧 2. Easter in the United Kingdom

🇬🇧

United Kingdom

SPRING 🌷

Popular food: hot cross buns – sweet, spiced buns with a cross on top. They are traditionally eaten on Good Friday.

Chocolate eggs: Children receive chocolate eggs and sometimes take part in Easter egg hunts.

Egg rolling: In some places, people roll decorated or hard-boiled eggs down a hill. The winner may be the person whose egg travels the furthest without breaking.

Easter Sunday: Many families enjoy a special meal together. Roast lamb is a traditional choice in some homes.

Other traditions: Easter cards, spring flowers, church services and family walks.

⭐ Fun fact: Hot cross buns have a cross on top. The cross is connected with the Christian story of Good Friday.

🇺🇸 3. Easter in the United States

🇺🇸

United States of America

SPRING 🌷

Easter Bunny: The Easter Bunny is a popular character. Children often receive baskets filled with chocolate eggs, jelly beans and other sweets.

Easter egg hunt: Adults hide colourful eggs in gardens, parks or inside houses. Children look for them and collect them in baskets.

White House Easter Egg Roll: Every year, children and families can take part in a special Easter celebration at the White House in Washington, D.C. One of its famous activities is rolling eggs across the lawn.

Easter food: Families may enjoy ham, potatoes, vegetables, cakes and chocolate. Traditions vary from family to family.

Other traditions: Easter parades, church services, spring decorations and family gatherings.

⭐ Fun fact: The White House Easter Egg Roll tradition dates back to 1878!

🇦🇺 4. Easter in Australia

🇦🇺

Australia

AUTUMN 🍂

Easter Bilby: Australia has a special Easter character called the Easter Bilby. The bilby is a native Australian marsupial with long ears. Chocolate bilbies are sold as an alternative to chocolate bunnies.

Why a bilby? Rabbits are introduced animals in Australia and can cause environmental problems. The bilby is a native animal, and chocolate bilbies can help raise awareness of its conservation.

Hot cross buns: Australians enjoy hot cross buns, especially around Easter.

Long weekend: Easter is a popular time for family trips, camping, outdoor activities and gatherings.

Other traditions: Easter egg hunts, chocolate eggs, family meals and community events.

⭐ Fun fact: The Easter Bilby is a real animal-inspired character – not just another name for the Easter Bunny!

🇮🇪 5. Easter in Ireland

🇮🇪

Ireland

SPRING 🌷

Religious traditions: Easter is an important Christian celebration in Ireland. Many people attend church services during Holy Week and Easter Sunday.

Eggs and chocolate: Children enjoy chocolate eggs and Easter egg hunts. Families may give Easter eggs as presents.

Easter Monday: It is a public holiday in the Republic of Ireland. People may relax, visit family or enjoy outdoor activities.

Traditional customs: In the past, Irish Easter celebrations included decorated eggs, special meals, new clothes and community gatherings. Some local folk customs are still remembered today.

Food: Roast lamb, potatoes and seasonal foods may be served for a special family meal. Hot cross buns are also known in Ireland.

⭐ Fun fact: In the past, some Irish communities had outdoor Easter Monday fairs with games, music and dancing.

🇳🇿 6. Easter in New Zealand

🇳🇿

New Zealand

AUTUMN 🍂

Autumn Easter: Easter comes in autumn in New Zealand. The weather is often cooler than in spring celebrations in the Northern Hemisphere.

Hot cross buns: These spiced buns with a cross are a popular Easter food, especially on Good Friday.

Chocolate eggs: Children enjoy chocolate eggs, Easter Bunny treats and egg hunts.

Family time: Easter is a long weekend for many people. Families may travel, visit friends or enjoy outdoor activities.

Religious traditions: Churches hold services during Holy Week and on Easter Sunday.

⭐ Fun fact: In New Zealand, Easter is an autumn holiday. You can enjoy chocolate eggs while the leaves are changing colour!

🇨🇦 7. Easter in Canada

🇨🇦

Canada

SPRING 🌷

Easter egg hunts: Children look for hidden chocolate eggs and sweets in gardens, parks and homes.

Easter Bunny: The Easter Bunny is a popular character in Canadian Easter celebrations.

Good Friday and Easter Monday: Good Friday is a statutory holiday for many workers. Easter Monday is a holiday for some employees, but rules differ between workplaces and provinces.

Food: Families may enjoy ham, roast meals, chocolate eggs, hot cross buns and other festive treats.

Québec: In French-speaking Québec, people may use the greeting “Joyeuses Pâques!” which means “Happy Easter!”

⭐ Fun fact: Canada is a very large country, so Easter weather can be different from one region to another. Some places may still have snow!

📖 10. Reading – Easter Around the World

Six Countries, One Celebration

Easter is celebrated in many English-speaking countries. People have different traditions, but chocolate eggs and family time are popular in many places.

In the United Kingdom, children enjoy Easter egg hunts. Families may eat hot cross buns on Good Friday. Some people also roll decorated eggs down hills.

In the United States, the Easter Bunny brings baskets with sweets and chocolate eggs. Children look for hidden eggs in gardens. The White House has a famous Easter Egg Roll every year.

In Australia, Easter takes place in autumn. There is a special character called the Easter Bilby. The bilby is a native animal, and chocolate bilbies help people learn about wildlife conservation.

In Ireland, Easter is an important religious celebration. Families may go to church, share a special meal and enjoy chocolate eggs. Easter Monday is a public holiday.

New Zealand also celebrates Easter in autumn. Hot cross buns are popular, and many families enjoy a long weekend together.

In Canada, children enjoy Easter egg hunts and chocolate treats. Some families in Québec say “Joyeuses Pâques!” at Easter.

Although the weather and customs are different, Easter is a time of celebration, family and new beginnings in all these countries.
