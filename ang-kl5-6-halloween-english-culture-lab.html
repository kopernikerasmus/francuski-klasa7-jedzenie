🎃 1. What is Halloween?

Halloween is celebrated every year on 31 October. It is popular in the United States, Canada, Ireland, the United Kingdom and many other countries.

People decorate their homes with pumpkins, ghosts, bats and skeletons. Children often wear costumes and go from house to house asking for sweets. Halloween is spooky, but it is also playful and creative!

Important! Halloween is on 31 October. All Saints’ Day is on 1 November, and All Souls’ Day is on 2 November. These dates have different traditions and meanings.

📜 2. A little bit of history

🔥 Ancient Samhain

Over 2,000 years ago, Celtic people in Ireland and parts of Britain celebrated Samhain at the end of the harvest season. It marked the beginning of winter.

⛪ All Hallows’ Eve

The evening before All Saints’ Day was called All Hallows’ Eve. Over time, the name became Halloween.

🎃 Jack-o’-lantern

Irish people told stories about a man called Jack. They carved turnips to make lanterns. In America, pumpkins became popular because they were bigger and easier to carve.

Halloween developed over many centuries. Modern celebrations combine older customs with newer games, decorations and popular culture.

🕸️ 3. Halloween traditions

🍬 Trick-or-treating

Children dress up and visit neighbours. They say “Trick or treat!” and receive sweets or other treats. It is a playful request for something nice to eat.

🎃 Pumpkin carving

People cut a face into a pumpkin, take out the seeds and put a small light inside. This decoration is called a jack-o’-lantern.

🧛 Costumes and fancy-dress parties

People dress up as witches, vampires, ghosts, superheroes, animals or characters from stories and films. In British English, a costume party is often called a fancy-dress party.

🏚️ Haunted houses and spooky stories

Some people visit attractions decorated like haunted houses. Friends may tell ghost stories or watch not-too-scary films.

🍎 Apple bobbing

A traditional game is apple bobbing. Apples float in water and players try to catch one using only their mouths. Always play safely and with adult supervision.

Culture tip: Not everyone celebrates Halloween. Some families enjoy costumes and parties; others prefer not to take part. It is important to respect different choices.

📖 5. Reading – My Halloween Party (A2)

Hi! My name is Alex and Halloween is my favourite autumn celebration.

On 31 October, my friends come to my house for a Halloween party. This year I am wearing a vampire costume. My sister is a witch and my best friend is a funny skeleton.

We decorate the living room with orange balloons, paper bats and a big jack-o’-lantern. My dad makes pumpkin soup, and we have lots of sweets and popcorn.

We play a guessing game and tell spooky stories. At eight o’clock, we watch a funny Halloween film. It is not too scary!

My favourite part is the pumpkin. We make its face together and put a little light inside. Happy Halloween!

🌟 8. Did you know?

  • The word Halloween comes from All Hallows’ Eve.
  • Orange and black are traditional Halloween colours.
  • Orange is linked with autumn and harvest.
  • Black is linked with night and mystery.
  • In some places, people decorate their homes weeks before 31 October.
